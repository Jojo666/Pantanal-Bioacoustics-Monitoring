# Pantanal-Bioacoustics-Monitoring
🌿 Pantanal Bioacoustic Monitoring: Wildlife Species Identification

How do you protect an ecosystem you can’t fully see? One way is to listen.This repository contains the development of machine learning models designed to automatically identify wildlife species from their vocalizations. By leveraging Passive Acoustic Monitoring (PAM), we aim to provide a scalable solution for monitoring the Pantanal—one of the world's most diverse yet threatened ecosystems.📍 OverviewThe Pantanal spans over 150,000 $km^2$ across Brazil and neighboring countries. It is home to over 650 bird species and countless other animals. However, the region faces immense pressure from:🔥 Wildfires and agricultural expansion🌊 Seasonal flooding and climate change🛰️ Logistics: The sheer scale makes manual fieldwork and data review impossible.🎯 The ChallengeConventional monitoring is expensive and logistically demanding. While a network of 1,000 acoustic recorders is being deployed to capture continuous soundscapes, the volume of data is too vast for human ears.The goal: Build robust ML models that can:Identify species within "messy" field-collected audio.Generalize across different habitats and seasons.Operate effectively even with limited labeled training data.🛠️ Proposed SolutionThis project focuses on turning raw audio recordings into actionable biodiversity insights. Key technical focus areas include:Feature Extraction: Converting audio into spectrograms for visual pattern recognition.Robustness: Developing architectures that can handle environmental noise (wind, rain, interference).Efficiency: Creating models capable of processing thousands of hours of audio to support evidence-based conservation.📁 Repository Structuredata/: Scripts for downloading or preprocessing the Pantanal audio datasets.notebooks/: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and experimental model training.src/: Core logic for audio processing, augmentation, and inference.models/: Saved model weights and architecture definitions.🚀 Getting Started
